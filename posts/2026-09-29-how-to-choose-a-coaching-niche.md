You do not have to stop being good at four things. Your homepage has to stop saying so.

That is the whole problem, and it is worth separating from the choice itself. Plenty of the coaches I read are not undecided about who they help at all. They are decided about four groups, and trying to write one page that serves all four.

So the page ends up being the average. And nobody recognises the average as themselves.

There are [117 coaching niches with buyer evidence behind them](/blog/2026-09-29-coaching-niches), and 918 subniches inside those. If you are good at several, you are not indecisive. You are normal.

## Which one do you pick?

The one where you can quote the buyer without inventing the quote.

That is the test, and it is faster than any exercise involving a grid. Take each of your four. For each one, write down the sentence that was going through someone's head on the morning they went looking for help. Not what they need. What they were thinking.

For one of the four, you will write it straight out, because you have heard someone say it. For the others you will start reaching for adjectives, or you will write what you think they should be feeling.

Pick the one you could quote.

## What happens to the other three?

Nothing. They stay in the work.

A niche is a door, not a fence. It decides what the homepage says, which decides who walks in.

But what happens in the room afterwards is between you and the client, and plenty of that will be the other three things.

I have read coaches who pick a niche and then write as though they have signed a contract never to mention anything else. That is not what it is for. The page has one job, which is getting the right stranger to believe you understand their particular problem. Once they are a client, the narrowness has done its work.

## Does this mean building four websites?

No. It means one page that is specific, and everything else staying where it is.

The version that actually costs you money is the reverse: four things listed on one homepage with equal weight, so a visitor has to work out which one you are best at. Some will bother. The rest close the tab.

If you genuinely serve two markets that never overlap, two pages is reasonable. Four is a business with no front door.

## What if I pick wrong?

You will not have picked wrong, you will have picked first.

Feeling sure and being right are not the same thing, which is why this is worth treating as a test rather than a vow. Write the page to one market. Leave it three months. If nobody in that market recognises themselves, you have learned something you could not have reasoned your way to.

The cost of changing a homepage is an afternoon. And the cost of not choosing is every visitor who could not tell what you do.

## Go one level down before you commit

Almost every niche splits. Of the 918 subniches we mapped, 733 want something different from the niche they sit inside.

So "anxiety" is not a choice yet. Postnatal anxiety is. Social anxiety is. They want different things, from different drives, and the same page cannot speak to both.

Pick the market, then pick the room inside it.

## Where the evidence stops

This is about what a page says, not about what you are qualified to do. Nothing here is advice on scope of practice or on which clients you should take.

The 117 and the 918 come from markets where buyers already spend money on books, so a market with no publishing behind it will not appear in them. That does not mean it has no buyers. It means we had nothing to read.

## FAQ

### Can I have more than one coaching niche?

In your practice, yes. On one page, no. A page speaking to two markets speaks to the average of them, and nobody is the average.

### How narrow is too narrow?

When you cannot find fifteen people talking about the problem in public, in their own words. If the conversation is not happening anywhere you can read it, the market is probably too small to find you.

### Do I have to put my niche in my job title?

No. It has to be in the first thing a stranger reads on the page, which is usually the headline. Whether it is also in your title is a branding question, not a search one.

### What if I have no clients yet and cannot quote anyone?

Then read instead. Fifteen reviews of the best-selling books in the market will give you the buyer's own words, which is where our own research started.
