Here are the coaching industry statistics we've gathered ourselves. We scored 10,954 live coaching websites out of 10, and the average was 3.7. 86% can't be understood by a stranger in five seconds, 82% show no price, and about two thirds show no proof at all. We've also mapped 918 coaching markets across 117 niches and scored 11,377 coaching LinkedIn headlines.

Every figure below links to the article where we talk about it properly. Where I've used somebody else's numbers, I've said so and linked the source.

## Things to know

- The average live coaching website scores 3.7 out of 10, and only 13 out of 10,954 scored strong.
- 86% of coaching websites fail the five-second test. At a softer pass mark it's still 50%.
- 82% of coaching websites show no price, but 65% of the sites that scored well do show one.
- Technical health averages 9 out of 10, so the build is rarely the problem. The words are.
- The ICF puts the number of coach practitioners worldwide at 122,974 in its 2025 study.

What does that tell you? That coaching websites are well built and badly worded. They load, they work on a phone and they're secure. What they don't do is tell a stranger who they're for, show that the coaching works, or say what it costs.

If you only take one number from this page, take the 86%. A stranger arrives, can't tell in five seconds whether you're for them, and goes back to the search results. That's the single biggest problem we found, and it's got nothing to do with how good anybody is at coaching.

## How big is the coaching industry?

These aren't our numbers. They come from the International Coaching Federation, the industry body, and I can't check them the way I can check ours, so treat them as their estimate.

The ICF's 2025 Global Coaching Study, run with PwC, says the number of coach practitioners worldwide reached a record 122,974, and that coaching brought in an estimated $5.34 billion in revenue. The survey had over 10,000 participants in 127 countries, according to the [ICF's announcement of the 2025 study](https://coachingfederation.org/blog/coaching-industry-continues-global-growth-with-5-34-billion-usd-revenue-new-research-reveals/). The same release says 59% of coaches expect their revenue to grow next year.

Two things I'd say about that. First, those are survey figures, so they depend on who answered. Second, if you do the maths, $5.34 billion across 122,974 practitioners isn't a huge amount per person, and plenty of coaches will be earning well below any average. I don't know the spread, and the ICF's headline figures don't give it.

## Coaching industry trends

I'll be honest here. Our own figures are a snapshot, so on their own they can't tell you a trend. When we score the same sites again later, we'll be able to say what's changed.

What I can tell you is what the snapshot points to. Coaches have mostly sorted out the build, with technical health averaging 9 out of 10. The next thing to sort out is the words: who it's for, what changes, proof and a price. That's where the marks are being lost, and that's also what [marketing for coaches](/blog/marketing-for-coaches) comes back to across all our data.

On technology, the ICF says only 19% of coaches invested in new technology such as AI in the past year, according to its [release on the 2025 study's regional findings](https://coachingfederation.org/blog/global-coaching-industry-surpasses-5-34-billion-as-emerging-markets-drive-growth/). Make of that what you will. In my opinion a coach doesn't need new technology to fix a homepage headline.

## How we got these numbers

Nothing fancy, and I'd rather you knew exactly what's behind them.

For the websites, we read the homepage of each live coaching site and scored it out of 10 on ten things a stranger needs:

- technical health (does it load, work on a phone and feel secure)
- a clear next step
- a way to sign up or stay in touch
- the five-second read (can a stranger say what you do and who for)
- being specific about who you help and what you fix
- how clear the offer is
- signs the coach knows their stuff
- a story, or anything human on the page
- whether a price is shown
- proof, like testimonials or results

We only scored the homepage, and only sites that were live. Sites that wouldn't load, or loaded with nothing readable, were left out rather than scored zero, because scoring them zero would have dragged the average down and made the market look worse than it is.

For the markets, we read the books buyers pay for and the things real buyers write about their problems, then grouped them into 117 niches and 918 markets underneath. If nobody is publishing books in a market, it won't show up, so the count is a floor rather than a ceiling.

For LinkedIn, we scored 11,377 headlines from people whose LinkedIn title says they coach.

None of this sees what happens on a discovery call, in a referral or in the coaching itself. A coach with a low-scoring homepage and a full diary absolutely exists. The data shows what a stranger meets, which is the part you can do something about. If you want to see where your own homepage sits, you can [score it against the 10,954](/website).

## FAQ

### How many life coaches are there in the world?

The ICF's 2025 Global Coaching Study puts the number of coach practitioners worldwide at a record 122,974. It's a survey estimate from the industry body, and people who coach without calling it that won't all be counted.

### How big is the coaching industry?

The ICF estimates coaching brought in $5.34 billion in revenue in its 2025 study, based on a survey of over 10,000 people in 127 countries. It's their estimate and we haven't checked it ourselves.

### What percentage of coaching websites show their prices?

Very few. When we scored 10,954 live coaching websites, 82% showed no price at all. Of the sites that scored well overall, 65% showed one.

### What is the average score for a coaching website?

3.7 out of 10, with a median of 4, across 10,954 live coaching websites scored on ten things a stranger needs. Only 13 scored strong, and 94% came out weak or poor.
