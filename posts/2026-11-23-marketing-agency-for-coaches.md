A marketing agency for coaches can do the work you don't have time for: ads, posts, emails, a new website. What it can't do is decide who you help and what you fix. If that isn't clear yet, an agency will spend your money sending more people to a message that doesn't work. Get the message right first, then decide if you need help pushing it out.

![A man with his hand on his chin looking unsure at a laptop while two younger people show him something in a cafe](marketing-agency-for-coaches.jpg)

## Things to know

- An agency does the doing. You still have to know who you help, their problem and your offer.
- Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, 85.7% fail a five-second test of "who is this for?". More traffic won't fix that.
- Agencies usually charge a monthly fee, often with a minimum term. Read the contract.
- Ask what they'll measure. Followers aren't clients.
- For a lot of coaches starting out, one channel done well beats paying someone to do five.

## What does a marketing agency for coaches actually do?

Usually some mix of these:

- social media posts and scheduling
- paid ads on Facebook, Instagram or Google
- email newsletters and sequences
- a website or landing pages
- search (SEO) work

Some sell "marketing packages for coaches", a fixed bundle at a fixed monthly price. That's easier to budget for, but check what's actually in it.

## When an agency won't help

When the message is the problem. I've worked with coaches since 2007, and the pattern is the same. A coach isn't getting clients, so they pay for more marketing. The marketing sends more people to their page. Those people don't understand what's on offer, so they leave. The coach blames the agency, and the agency blames the ads.

The numbers say the same thing. Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, the average is 3.65 out of 10. 85.7% fail a five-second test of "who is this for and what does it fix?". 66.5% show no proof at all. An agency that sends traffic to a page like that is pouring water into a leaky bucket.

So before you hire anyone, check three things on your own page:

1. Would a stranger know in five seconds who you help and what you fix?
2. Is there proof that you've helped someone like them?
3. Is there one clear next step?

If the answer to any is no, fix that first. [What to put on a coaching homepage](/blog/what-to-put-on-a-coaching-homepage) and [the five-second test](/blog/five-second-test) will help.

## When an agency can help

When your message works and you're short of time. If people who find you already turn into clients, and you just need more of them, handing the doing to someone else makes sense. The same goes for skilled work like running paid ads, which can burn money fast if you don't know what you're doing.

## What to ask a marketing agency before you sign

- **Who else in coaching have you worked with, and what happened?** Ask for results in clients or calls booked, not likes.
- **What will you measure each month?** Calls booked and clients signed are the numbers to ask for.
- **Who writes the words?** If it's them, how will they learn how your buyers talk?
- **How long is the contract, and how do I leave?** Avoid long lock-ins until they've proved themselves.
- **What do you need from me?** An agency still needs your time, especially at the start.

## What does a marketing agency for coaches cost?

It varies a lot by country, by what's included and by whether ad spend is on top. Ad spend is usually extra, paid to the ad platform, on top of the agency's fee. Work out what a new client is worth to you first. If a client pays you £1,200, you know roughly what you can afford to spend to get one.

## What are the other options?

- **Do one thing yourself, properly.** One channel, done consistently, often beats an agency doing five half-heartedly. [Marketing for coaches](/blog/marketing-for-coaches) lays out the options.
- **Hire a freelancer for one job.** An ads specialist, an email writer, a web designer. Cheaper than a full agency and easier to judge.
- **Get help with the plan, not the posting.** Some coaches need someone to sort the message and the plan, then do the posting themselves. A [life coach marketing plan](/blog/life-coach-marketing-plan) is a good place to start.
- **A coach for your coaching business.** [Business coach for coaches](/blog/business-coach-for-coaches) covers when that's worth it.

## FAQ

### Do coaches need a marketing agency?

Not usually at the start. An agency is worth it when your message already works and you're short of time. If people don't understand what you offer, fix that first, or the agency just sends more people to a page that doesn't convert.

### What should a marketing agency for coaches do?

Whatever your plan needs: ads, social posts, emails, a website or search work. A good one also tracks calls booked and clients signed, not just followers and likes.

### How much does a marketing agency for coaches cost?

It depends on the country, what's included and whether ad spend is extra. Agencies usually charge monthly. Compare the cost against what one new client is worth to you.

### What are marketing packages for coaches?

Fixed bundles of marketing work, like a set number of posts, emails or ads each month, for a fixed fee. Check exactly what's included and what results they promise to measure.
