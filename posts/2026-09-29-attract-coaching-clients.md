Every coach has been told to attract clients rather than chase them, usually by someone who then sells them a DM script.

Attraction turns out to be measurable, so here it is measured, from 918 coaching markets we mapped from 2,004 books and 1,547 things real buyers wrote, and 10,954 coaching websites we scored on whether a stranger can follow them. It's the order [the three jobs of getting clients](/blog/how-to-get-coaching-clients) happen in, more than anything else.

Start with the one that surprised me most. Scarcity and urgency, the two-spots-left, the price-goes-up-Friday, support the buying decision in 4.4% of the 918 markets.

It's the most taught tactic in coaching and it's the one the evidence backs least.

## Things to know

- Urgency supports the sale in 4.4% of the 918 markets we mapped. Social proof does in 92.9%.
- 49.9% of markets are driven by relief from something that hurts. Not ambition.
- Only 14.3% of coaching websites can be understood in five seconds. Recognition can't happen before understanding.
- 38.9% of buyers can name their problem and can't name the solution. Anything that sounds like selling arrives too early for them.

## What's the actual difference between attracting and chasing?

Chasing is asking before she's recognised anything. Attracting is being recognisable before you ask.

Both halves are measurable, in two places. Whether your first sentence describes a problem she'd agree she has, and whether anything on the page lets her check you out before a conversation starts.

Look at what the markets we mapped actually turn on.

| What the decision turns on | Share of 918 markets |
|---|---|
| Social proof | 92.9% |
| Authority, the sense you've seen this before | 87.9% |
| Belonging, somebody who's been where I am | 43.5% |
| Liking | 38.1% |
| Consistency with something she's already started | 27.5% |
| Scarcity and urgency | 4.4% |
| Reciprocity, the free thing first | 3.4% |

The top three are all forms of recognition. Somebody like me did this, somebody who understands it is telling me, somebody who's been here. None of them involve asking for anything.

The bottom two are the tactics. Countdown timers and free PDFs. They show up in 4.4% and 3.4% of markets.

## Why does pushing harder make it worse?

Because in 38.9% of markets she hasn't decided coaching is the answer yet, and a hard ask tells her you weren't listening.

We tagged all 918 markets for how far along the buyer is. 1.3% don't know they've got a problem. 38.9% know what's wrong and have no idea what fixes it. 52.2% know the kind of solution they want and are comparing.

Those need different first lines and only one of them can handle an offer up front. Send a booking link to somebody in that 38.9% and you've answered a question she hasn't asked.

Then there's what she's actually buying. In 49.9% of the markets we mapped, the drive underneath the purchase is getting away from something that hurts. A woman who hasn't slept properly since February, a man who dreads Sunday evenings, a mother who can't say the thing she needs to say, a manager who's been found out by his own promotion. None of them want to grow. They want out.

Urgency aimed at a person in that state reads as pressure from somebody who doesn't get it. Naming what she's living reads as the first person who does.

## So how do you make somebody recognise themselves?

By describing the problem before you describe yourself, in the words she'd use about it.

That's the whole mechanism, and the site data says almost nobody does it. Only 14.3% of the 10,954 coaching websites we scored can be understood in five seconds by a stranger. The ones that can score 9.25 out of 10 on it. The ones that can't score 2.92. Nothing else we measure separates them by anything like that much.

Recognition can't happen before comprehension. If she can't work out what you do, there's nothing for her to see herself in.

Three things do the work, in this order.

**The problem, in her language, above anything about you.** [Why the five-second read decides so much](/blog/five-second-test) has the evidence.

**Proof she can check without talking to you.** 66.5% of coaching websites show none, in a market where proof supports the decision 92.9% of the time.

**A way to stay near you without committing.** 18.2% of coaching sites ask for a booking and give her no other option. She's either ready today or lost.

## Is attraction marketing just waiting?

No, and that's the version that gets coaches into trouble.

Being recognisable still means publishing, showing up, asking people to have conversations. What changes is the order. You earn recognition first and ask second, rather than asking a hundred strangers and calling the silence a numbers game.

Does urgency ever work? Yes, in the 4.4% of markets where the deadline is real, which usually means the thing itself has a date on it. An exam, a wedding, a court hearing, a due date. When the deadline belongs to her life rather than your funnel, it's not a tactic.

## What this can't tell you

Our data covers what buyers respond to and what coaching websites show them. It doesn't cover what you say on a call, and that's where the sale usually happens.

It also can't prove the direction. A site with proof on it might be a site built by a coach who already had clients. All we can say is what the markets turn on and what the pages contain, and the distance between the two is large.

## FAQ

### How do I attract coaching clients instead of chasing them?

By describing her problem in her own words before you describe yourself, and by giving her something to check before she has to talk to you. Proof supports the buying decision in 92.9% of the markets we mapped. Urgency does in 4.4%.

### Does urgency work for selling coaching?

Rarely. It supports the decision in 4.4% of the 918 markets we mapped, and the ones where it does are markets with a real date in them: an exam, a birth, a hearing. Inventing a deadline in the other 95.6% reads as pressure.

### Why is nobody enquiring through my coaching website?

86% of coaching websites fail on whether a stranger can tell what they do, and 66.5% show no proof at all. If both are true of yours, the enquiries were never going to come, however much traffic you send.

### What's the difference between attraction marketing and just waiting?

The order, not the effort. You still publish and still ask people for conversations. You earn the recognition first, rather than asking strangers who've got no reason to say yes yet.
