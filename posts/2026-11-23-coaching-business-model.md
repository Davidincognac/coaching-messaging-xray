A coaching business model is simply how you turn your time and know-how into money. There are five common ones: one-to-one sessions, one-to-one packages, group coaching, online courses and memberships. A lot of coaches end up mixing two of them, but almost everyone should start with one-to-one, because it's where you learn what your buyers actually want.

![A woman in her fifties at a desk sketching three columns of boxes in a notebook, with a cup of coffee beside her](coaching-business-model.jpg)

## Things to know

- One-to-one is the easiest model to start and the hardest to grow, because your hours run out.
- Group coaching and courses only work once you know what people need. That knowledge comes from one-to-one.
- Your model decides how many clients you need. The sum is income plus costs, divided by price.
- Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, 82.3% show no price at all. Whatever your model, say what it costs.
- An "online coaching business model" is one of these five, run over video and email. Online is where, not what.

## The five coaching business models

### 1. One-to-one sessions

You sell single sessions, paid one at a time. It's simple and it's how a lot of coaches start. The problem is that clients come and go, and you never know what next month looks like. Pay-per-session also invites people to stop as soon as they feel a bit better, before the real change happens.

### 2. One-to-one packages

You sell a set number of sessions over a set time, for one price. Six sessions over ten weeks, say. This is the model I'd point new coaches at. The client commits to finishing, you know your income for the next few months, and the result is something people can picture. [Life coaching packages](/blog/coaching-packages) goes into how to build them.

### 3. Group coaching

You coach several people at once, usually on a call each week. One hour of your time earns several fees, so it grows better than one-to-one. But a group needs people with the same problem at the same time, and you need enough of them to fill it. If you can't fill a one-to-one diary yet, you won't fill a group. [Group coaching programs](/blog/group-coaching-program) covers how to run one.

### 4. Online courses

You record what you'd teach, and people buy it and work through it alone. It earns without your time once it's made, which is why it's sold so hard to coaches. The catch is that courses sell on volume, and volume needs an audience you probably haven't got yet. Completion rates on self-paced courses also tend to be low, so the result people get is weaker.

### 5. Memberships

People pay monthly for ongoing access, usually a community, regular calls and some content. Steady monthly money is the attraction. The work is keeping people paying month after month, which means a steady flow of new stuff and a reason to stay.

## Which coaching business model should you start with?

One-to-one packages, for almost everyone. Here's why.

You learn what your buyers say, what they struggle with and what they'll pay for. That's the raw material for everything else. A group programme, a course or a membership built before you've coached real people is built on guesses.

Then grow in this order: packages first, then a group for the people who want it cheaper, then maybe a course for the people who want to do it themselves. Each step uses what you learned in the one before.

## How the money works in each model

Here's the same made-up coach under three models. She wants £40,000 a year and her costs are £2,000 a year, so she needs £42,000 coming in.

- **Packages at £1,200:** £42,000 ÷ £1,200 = 35 clients a year. About 3 a month.
- **Group at £400 a head, 8 per group:** £3,200 per group, so about 13 groups a year. That's 105 people.
- **Course at £150:** £42,000 ÷ £150 = 280 sales a year.

Look at what each one needs. Three clients a month is a handful of conversations a week. 280 course sales a year needs a lot of people seeing your stuff. That's why I say packages first. The numbers are made up, but the shape of the sum is the same for everyone. The [coaching business plan](/blog/coaching-business-plan) walks through the full sum.

## What about the online coaching business model?

It's the same five models, run over video, email and messaging instead of in a room. Being online means you can coach anyone in your time zone, and your costs are lower. It doesn't change which model works. [How to start an online coaching business](/blog/how-to-start-an-online-coaching-business) covers the setup.

## What do the coaches who do well have in common?

From the sites we've scored, the coaches who get the best marks say who they help, what they fix and what it costs, and they show proof. The model sits behind all that. A clear one-to-one package with a price and three testimonials will beat a shiny membership that nobody understands.

## FAQ

### What is a coaching business model?

It's how you exchange your coaching for money. The common ones are single sessions, packages, group coaching, online courses and memberships. Each has a different price, a different number of clients needed and a different amount of your time.

### Which coaching business model makes the most money?

The one that suits where you are. Group coaching, courses and memberships can earn more per hour, but only once you've got enough people. For a coach starting out, one-to-one packages usually bring in money soonest.

### Can I combine coaching business models?

Yes. A common mix is one-to-one packages plus a group programme, or packages plus a low-priced course. Start with one, get it working, then add the second.

### Is an online coaching business model different?

Not really. It's the same models delivered online. It lowers your costs and widens where your clients can come from, but you still have to pick which model you're running.
