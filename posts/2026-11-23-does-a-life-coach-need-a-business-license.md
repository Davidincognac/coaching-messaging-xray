No, not a coaching license. Life coaching isn't a licensed profession in the US, so no state requires a license to call yourself a life coach. But that's not the whole answer. Your city, county or state may still want a general business license for any business run there, and if your coaching strays into therapy, medical or investment advice, you're into work that does need a license.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules change from place to place, so check with your city or county office before you start.

## Things to know

- There's no state license for life coaches in the US.
- Many cities and counties ask every business, including a one-person coaching business, for a general business license.
- If you trade under a name that isn't your own, you'll usually need to register it (often called a DBA, "doing business as").
- Therapy, medical advice and investment advice are licensed. Coaching that crosses into them can get you into trouble.
- In the UK, coaching isn't licensed either. A lot of coaches start as a sole trader and register for Self Assessment.

## Why isn't life coaching licensed?

Because nobody has made it a regulated job. Therapists, counsellors, doctors and financial advisers are licensed because what they do is treated as risky to the public. Coaching isn't. There are coaching bodies that offer credentials, but those are voluntary. They're not a legal requirement.

That's good news for starting, and a warning at the same time. Anyone can call themselves a coach, which is why buyers look hard at proof. Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, 66.5% show no proof at all. Without a license to point at, testimonials and results are what people trust.

## What licenses or registrations might you still need?

Here are the usual ones. Not all of them will apply to you.

**A general business license.** Some cities and counties want every business registered, even one run from a spare room. Some don't. The US Small Business Administration has a guide on [applying for licenses and permits](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits) that explains federal, state and local ones.

**A DBA.** If you coach as "Brave Steps Coaching" rather than under your own name, you'll usually need to file that name with your state or county.

**A home business permit.** Some places want one if you run a business from home, especially if clients visit.

**An EIN.** As a sole proprietor with no staff you can often use your own Social Security number. Some coaches get an EIN anyway to keep it off invoices. The IRS explains it on its [EIN page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number).

**An LLC.** Not required. Some coaches form one to keep business and personal money apart. Ask an accountant if it's worth it for you.

## Where does coaching cross into licensed work?

This is the part that can actually hurt you. A few examples:

- **Mental health.** Helping someone set goals is coaching. Treating depression, anxiety or trauma is therapy, and that needs a license.
- **Health.** Helping someone stick to habits is coaching. Diagnosing conditions or prescribing diets for a medical condition is not.
- **Money.** Helping someone build a budget is coaching. Telling them which investments to buy can be regulated investment advice.

A simple test: if you're diagnosing, treating or recommending specific products for a regulated area, stop and check. Put it in your contract too. A line saying coaching isn't therapy or medical, legal or financial advice is standard. There's a [coaching contract template](/blog/coaching-contract-template) that includes one.

## What about life coaches in the UK?

Same idea. Coaching isn't regulated. A lot of coaches start as a sole trader, and the government says you [must register for Self Assessment as a sole trader](https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader) once you earn more than £1,000 from self-employment in a tax year. The same lines about therapy, health and money advice apply.

## So what should you actually do?

1. Call or check the website of your city or county clerk and ask what a home-based coaching business needs.
2. Register your business name if you're not using your own.
3. Keep your coaching clearly on the coaching side of the line, and say so in your contract.
4. Get professional liability insurance.

## FAQ

### Do you need a license to be a life coach?

Not a coaching license. Life coaching isn't a licensed profession in the US or UK. You may still need a general business license from your city or county, depending on where you live.

### Do life coaches need an LLC?

No. You can coach as a sole proprietor. An LLC is optional and some coaches use one to keep business and personal money separate. Talk to an accountant about whether it suits you.

### Can a life coach give mental health advice?

A life coach can help with goals, habits and decisions. Diagnosing or treating mental health conditions is therapy, which needs a license. If a client needs that, refer them to a licensed professional.

### Is a coaching certification the same as a license?

No. Certifications come from training bodies and are voluntary. A license is a legal permission from the government. Coaching has certifications but no license.
