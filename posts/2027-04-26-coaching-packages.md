A good coaching package tells a stranger exactly what they're buying: how long it lasts, how often you meet, what happens between sessions, what's included and what isn't, what it costs and how they can pay. You put one together by making those decisions one at a time, writing each answer down, and stopping when someone who's never met you could read it and know what saying yes means.

![A coach at a table comparing three handwritten package options laid side by side](coaching-packages-three-sheets.jpg)

The easiest way to show you is to build one. So below I'll take a made-up coach from "six sessions" to a finished package, one decision at a time, with the package written out as it changes.

## Why "six sessions" isn't a package

Think about booking a package holiday. You'd never pay for "seven nights somewhere warm". You want to know which hotel, which airport, whether it's half board or all inclusive, and what happens if you have to cancel.

Coaching gets sold like "seven nights somewhere warm" far too often. When we checked 10,954 live coaching websites, more than 8 in 10 of them showed no price at all. So the reader has to book a call just to find out what they'd get and what it costs, and in my opinion plenty of them won't bother.

## Meet Gemma (she's made up)

Gemma is a coach who helps parents go back to work after years at home raising their children. Like her, the prices below are made up.

The people she helps are real enough, though. We found 25 books written for people going back to work after a career break, and the people who bought them describe it very plainly. One wrote "I took a break from work to raise my family. I want to get back to working". Another talked about "the fear of going back to work after being unemployed for so long". Gemma's package has to deal with that fear.

Here's where Gemma starts:

> **Return to work coaching**
> 6 coaching sessions.

That's it, and to a stranger it says very little. If you haven't yet worked out who it's for and what changes for them, [how to put together a coaching offer](/blog/coaching-offer) covers that. Here we're building the package itself.

## Step 1: how long does it last?

The first decision is how long the whole thing runs, in weeks rather than sessions.

Gemma thinks about what actually has to happen for her clients. They need to work out what job they want, get their CV and their confidence back into shape, start applying and get through some interviews. In her experience that takes about three months for someone who's ready to start.

So she picks 12 weeks. That gives the package a start and a finish, and people can picture where they'll be at the end.

> **Return to work coaching**
> 12 weeks. 6 coaching sessions.

## Step 2: how often do you meet?

Six sessions over 12 weeks would mean one every fortnight. Gemma doesn't like that, because her clients are nervous at the start. Two weeks between the first and second session is a long time when you're scared, and that's when people talk themselves out of it.

So she changes the shape. Weekly sessions for the first four weeks while things are new and wobbly, then every two weeks after that while they're applying and need a bit of space to get on with it. That's eight sessions, not six, and each one is 60 minutes on video.

Think about when your own clients need you more and when they need you less.

> **Return to work coaching**
> 12 weeks. 8 one-to-one sessions of 60 minutes on video. Weekly for the first month, then every two weeks.

## Step 3: what happens between sessions?

This is the part people forget to say. Gemma's clients don't get nervous on a Tuesday at 10am when the session is booked. They get nervous on a Sunday night when they're about to send off an application, or the morning of an interview.

So Gemma makes the between-sessions help specific. Messages on weekdays, with a reply within one working day. One proper read-through of their CV with written notes. And one practice interview before their first real one.

Notice she doesn't say "unlimited support". It sounds generous, but nobody knows what it means, and it can turn into you answering messages at 11pm. Say what you'll actually do.

> **Return to work coaching**
> 12 weeks. 8 one-to-one sessions of 60 minutes on video. Weekly for the first month, then every two weeks.
> Between sessions: weekday messages, reply within one working day. A full CV read-through with written notes. One practice interview before your first real one.

## Step 4: what's included, and what isn't?

Saying what's not in the package is just as useful as what is. It stops arguments later.

Gemma writes down a few things she doesn't do. She won't write applications for people or find them jobs, because she's not a recruiter. She's also not a therapist or a doctor. If someone's been off work because of their mental health, she can coach them alongside the help they're getting, but coaches don't diagnose or treat anything, and she says so.

She also decides what happens with missed sessions, because it'll come up. Give 24 hours' notice and the session moves. Miss it without telling her and it can't be moved.

And she decides that all 8 sessions need to be used within the 12 weeks, so the package has a proper finish.

> **Return to work coaching**
> 12 weeks. 8 one-to-one sessions of 60 minutes on video. Weekly for the first month, then every two weeks.
> Between sessions: weekday messages, reply within one working day. A full CV read-through with written notes. One practice interview before your first real one.
> Not included: writing applications for you or finding you a job. This is coaching, not therapy or medical help.
> Moving a session: give 24 hours' notice and we'll rearrange. Miss a session without telling me and it can't be moved. All 8 sessions need to be used within the 12 weeks.

## Step 5: one package or three?

Next, Gemma wonders about offering a smaller and a bigger version too.

For now, she decides no. She's only been coaching for a couple of years, and one well-explained package is easier to sell and to deliver. She keeps one smaller option back, a single CV and interview session people can book on its own, but it sits underneath the main package, not next to it as an equal.

## Step 6: show the price or not?

Gemma decides to show it. In my opinion that's the right call for a package like this, because the work is roughly the same for everyone.

If your work really is different for every person, you can leave the price off, but say why and say what it depends on. [How to price your coaching](/blog/coaching-pricing) goes through both ways properly.

Gemma's made-up price is £1,350 for the 12 weeks. That's an example, not a number I'm telling you to charge.

## Step 7: payment plans

The last decision is how people can pay. Some of Gemma's clients have been living on one income for years, so £1,350 in one go is a lot, even if they can afford it over three months.

So she offers two ways. Pay £1,350 up front, or three monthly payments of £450, with the first one paid before the first session. She keeps the total the same either way. If you add interest or charge extra for paying in instalments, that can count as offering credit, which has its own rules in the UK and the US, so check the rules where you live first.

She also decides what happens if someone stops paying halfway. The sessions stop until the payment comes in.

> Price: £1,350 up front, or three monthly payments of £450. The first payment is made before the first session. If a payment is missed, sessions pause until it's paid.

## The finished package

Last of all she gives it a name that says the result, adds one line on who it's for, and says what to do next. Here's what Gemma ends up with.

> **Back to work in 12 weeks**
> For parents who took time out to raise a family and are nervous about going back.
>
> **What you get:** 8 one-to-one sessions of 60 minutes on video. Weekly for the first month while it's all new, then every two weeks while you're applying.
>
> **Between sessions:** message me on weekdays and I'll reply within one working day. A full read-through of your CV with written notes. One practice interview before your first real one.
>
> **Not included:** I won't write your applications or find you a job. This is coaching, not therapy or medical help.
>
> **Price:** £1,350, or three monthly payments of £450. The first payment is made before your first session. If a payment is missed, sessions pause until it's paid.
>
> **Moving a session:** give me 24 hours' notice and we'll rearrange. Miss a session without telling me and it can't be moved. All 8 sessions need to be used within the 12 weeks.
>
> **Next step:** book a 20-minute call to check it's right for you.
>
> *Want something smaller? Book a single CV and interview session on its own.*

Read that as a stranger. You'd know who it's for, what it costs and what to do next without having to ask.

That short call at the end is where the package gets tested, and [how to run a coaching discovery call](/blog/coaching-discovery-call) shows you what to say on it.

## Good, better, best: when three tiers help and when they don't

You'll have seen the three-tier idea, like bronze, silver and gold, with three boxes side by side and the middle one highlighted as the best value.

It helps when people really do want different amounts of help from you, and the difference between the tiers is something they can picture. For Gemma, that might look like:

- sessions only, for someone who's confident and just wants a sounding board
- sessions plus messages between, for someone who needs a bit more hand-holding
- all of that plus the CV read-through and two practice interviews, for someone who hasn't had an interview in ten years

Each one is for a different person, and a reader can see which one they are.

It just confuses people when the tiers are basically the same thing with different labels. "VIP access" and "priority support" don't tell anyone anything. If someone has to study three boxes to work out the difference, they'll often close the page and decide to think about it.

It also confuses things when you're new. If you haven't sold many packages yet, you don't know which version people want, so you're guessing three times instead of once. In my opinion, start with one clear package, sell it to a handful of people, listen to what they ask for, and then decide if you need a smaller or bigger version.

And if you do go with three, make sure each box answers the same questions in the same order: how long, how often, what's between sessions, what's included, what it costs. Otherwise people can't compare them properly.

Once people can see what they're buying, the next thing they want is to know it works for people like them. [How to get coaching testimonials people believe](/blog/coaching-testimonials) covers that.

## FAQ

### How many sessions should a coaching package have?

There isn't one right number. Work out how long the change takes for your clients first, then decide how often they need you during that time. Gemma ended up with eight because her clients need more help at the start, not because eight is a magic number.

### Should I sell single sessions as well as packages?

You can, but don't lead with them. A package has a start, an end and a result people can picture. A single session works well as a smaller option under the main package.

### Should a payment plan cost more than paying up front?

In my opinion, keep it the same. It's simpler and people trust it. If you do want to charge extra for paying in instalments, check the rules where you live first, because in the UK and the US that can count as offering credit.
