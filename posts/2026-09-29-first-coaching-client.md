Your first client is hard for one reason and it isn't skill. It's that the thing people need most before they hire a coach is evidence other people already did, and on day one you haven't got any.

Here's how big that problem really is, measured two ways: 918 coaching markets we mapped for what the buying decision turns on, and 10,954 live coaching websites we scored on whether they show any evidence at all. It's one piece of [the three jobs that get a stranger to book a call](/blog/how-to-get-coaching-clients), and it's the piece that hurts on day one.

Proof comes up in 92.9% of the 918 markets. It's the most common thing a buying decision hangs on, ahead of qualifications, ahead of price, ahead of everything.

Then you look at what coaches actually have on their sites. 66.5% show none.

## Things to know

- Proof decides the sale in 92.9% of the 918 markets we mapped. 66.5% of live coaching websites show none at all.
- The coaching sites that score well average 4.56 out of 10 on proof. Two testimonials puts you ahead of the market.
- 87.9% of markets also want authority, which isn't the same thing as a certificate.

## Why is the first one so much harder than the fifth?

Because the first one has to trust you on nothing, and the fifth one gets to read about the first four.

That's the whole asymmetry. Every coach feels it and most treat it as a confidence problem. It's an evidence problem, and evidence compounds, which is why coach number five feels like a different job.

Here's the part that should cheer you up. We scored all 10,954 sites out of 10 on proof, across the whole market and by tier.

| Proof score out of 10 | |
|---|---|
| Whole market average | 1.51 |
| The 646 sites that scored strong or decent | 4.56 |
| The 6,820 that scored poor | 0.78 |
| Share of the market showing no proof at all | 66.5% |

The good sites average 4.56 out of 10. That's not a wall of case studies and logos. That's a couple of named testimonials and something checkable.

You're not competing with a polished industry. 89.5% of the sites in the top group show some proof, 18.5% of the poor ones do, and two thirds of the whole market shows nothing. Two real quotes from two real people and you're past most of what a stranger will compare you against.

## What counts as proof before you've had a paying client?

Anything a stranger could check that isn't your own opinion of yourself.

- Somebody you coached for free, named, saying what changed. Free still happened.
- The thing you fixed in your own life, with the before and after in numbers or dates.
- Who you were before this. Fifteen years managing people is proof about managing people.
- A name a stranger recognises: where you trained, who you worked for, where you've been published.

That last one is doing a different job. 87.9% of the 918 markets also turn on authority, which is the sense that you've seen this problem before. A certificate is the weakest version of it. Having lived the exact thing she's living is the strongest, and it costs nothing to say.

## Does the certification help?

Less than the training provider implies, and it's not useless either.

Across the whole market, the thing we score as credibility, which covers qualifications and affiliations, averages 2.68 out of 10 and separates good sites from poor ones by 1.43 points. Proof separates them by 3.78. Being clear about who you help separates them by 6.33.

So the certificate is worth having and it won't get you the first client. What gets you the first client is being the obvious person for one specific problem, in front of somebody who has it. If you haven't settled that yet, that's [a niche question rather than a marketing one](/blog/coaching-niches).

## Where does the first one actually come from?

Somebody who already knows you, almost always.

I'm not going to dress that up. The first client comes from a warm list: people you've worked with, trained with, sat next to. Not because cold marketing can't work, but because warm people skip the proof step entirely. They already have their evidence, which is you.

## What this doesn't cover

Our data says what makes a stranger believe you. It says nothing about whether you can coach, and the two aren't related.

Is it possible to get a first client with no proof at all? Yes, constantly. Somebody takes a chance on you because you were in the room. The point of the numbers above is that you can stop treating the empty testimonial page as a reason to wait.

## FAQ

### How do I get my first coaching client with no experience?

From somebody who already knows you, and then you write down what happened so the second one has something to read. Proof decides the sale in 92.9% of the markets we mapped, and the fastest proof available to a beginner is one free client who'll say what changed.

### Should I coach for free to get started?

It's the cheapest way to buy the thing you're short of. One named person saying what changed puts you ahead of the 66.5% of coaching websites that show nothing, and you can't buy that with a course.

### How many testimonials do I need on my coaching website?

Fewer than you think. The coaching sites that score well average 4.56 out of 10 on proof, which in practice is two or three named quotes. The market average is 1.51.

### Can I use testimonials from clients I coached for free?

Yes, and you can say they were free. What a stranger needs is a named person saying what changed, not a receipt. 66.5% of coaching websites show nothing at all, so one real quote already puts you ahead of two thirds of the market.

### Does a coaching certification get me clients?

Not on its own. Credibility, which is where qualifications get counted, separates good coaching sites from poor ones by 1.43 points out of 10. Being clear about who you help separates them by 6.33.
