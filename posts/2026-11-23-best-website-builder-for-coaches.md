The best website builder for coaches is the one you'll actually keep up to date, that lets people book a call and pay you without fuss. For a lot of coaches that's a simple drag-and-drop builder. Coaches selling courses or memberships may want an all-in-one coaching platform. But the builder is the small decision. What decides whether a coaching website gets clients is what it says.

## Things to know

- Pick the builder on three things: can you edit it yourself, can people book, can people pay.
- Easy drag-and-drop builders suit coaches who want a simple site live quickly.
- All-in-one coaching platforms suit coaches selling courses, memberships or programs online.
- WordPress gives you full control, but you or someone else has to look after it.
- Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, the average is 3.65 out of 10. The weak marks come from the words, not the builder.

## What does a coaching website actually need?

Before you compare builders, list what your site has to do. For a one-to-one coach it's short:

- say who you help and what you fix, in the first screen
- show proof (testimonials, results)
- let people book a call
- take payment, or link to where they pay
- collect email addresses for people not ready yet
- look right on a phone

Any of the well-known builders does all of that. So the question is which one does it in a way you'll keep using.

## The main types of website builder for coaches

### Easy drag-and-drop builders

Squarespace and Wix are the well-known ones. You pick a template, drag things around and publish. They handle hosting, security and updates for you.

Good for: coaches who want a clean site live in a weekend and don't want to think about the technical side. For a simple coaching site, this is usually the easiest option.

Watch out for: templates that look lovely but push your message below big photos. Change the template to fit the words, not the other way round.

### All-in-one coaching platforms

Platforms like Kajabi bundle a website with courses, memberships, email and payments.

Good for: coaches selling online programs, courses or memberships who want it all in one place.

Watch out for: paying for features you don't use yet. If you only coach one-to-one, you may not need all of it.

### WordPress

The most flexible option, and it runs a huge share of the web. You can build almost anything.

Good for: coaches who want full control, or who have someone to look after it. It's strong for blogging and search.

Watch out for: you're responsible for hosting, updates, security and backups, or you pay someone to be.

### Design-led builders

Builders like Showit are popular with coaches who want a very visual, designed look.

Good for: coaches where looks are part of what they sell.

Watch out for: a beautiful site that's hard to read. Looks don't sell coaching on their own.

## How to choose in five minutes

1. **Selling courses or a membership?** Look at an all-in-one platform.
2. **Want to blog a lot and get found on Google?** WordPress is worth a look.
3. **Want something simple live fast?** An easy drag-and-drop builder.
4. **Can't decide?** Go with the easiest one you'll keep updated. You can move later.

## The builder isn't why coaching websites fail

We scored 10,954 live coaching websites. The average is 3.65 out of 10. 85.7% fail a five-second test of "who is this for and what does it fix?". 66.5% show no proof. 82.3% show no price.

None of those problems are caused by the builder. They're caused by what's written on the page. A coach on a basic builder with a clear headline and three testimonials will beat a coach on the fanciest platform with a headline that just says their name.

So spend an hour picking the builder and a week on the words. [What to put on a coaching homepage](/blog/what-to-put-on-a-coaching-homepage) gives you the order, and [website design for life coaches](/blog/website-design-for-life-coaches) shows what the high-scoring sites do differently.

## Do I need a website builder at all to start?

Not straight away. You can get your first clients without a website at all, through conversations, referrals and groups. [Coaching clients without a website](/blog/coaching-clients-without-a-website) covers how. A simple one-page site is enough once you're ready.

## FAQ

### What is the best website builder for coaches?

The one you'll keep updated that lets people book and pay easily. Easy drag-and-drop builders suit coaches who want simple, all-in-one platforms suit coaches selling courses or memberships, and WordPress suits those who want full control.

### What is the easiest website builder for a coaching business?

An easy drag-and-drop builder with ready-made templates, where hosting and updates are handled for you. You can usually get a simple coaching site live in a weekend.

### Do coaching website templates work?

Yes, as a starting point. Change the template to fit your words, not the other way round. Make sure your headline says who you help and what you fix, above any big photo.

### Should I use WordPress for my coaching website?

If you want full control and plan to blog a lot, it's a strong choice. You'll need to handle hosting, updates and security, or pay someone to.
