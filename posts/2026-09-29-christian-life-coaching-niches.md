Search for a Christian life coach and you'll get hundreds of people describing themselves exactly that way. Hardly any of them say what they'd actually help you with.

So this is what it costs them, and what belongs on the page instead.

Christian coaching works as a layer over a market rather than as a market of its own. We found that by reading what people buy on Amazon across [117 coaching markets](/blog/coaching-niches) and almost 1,000 smaller ones underneath. Faith came back as the deciding thing in six of them, and those six sit under six completely different parent markets.

That's important, because the man in one of them wants nothing like what the woman in another wants, and they'd both call themselves Christian.

## Things to know

- Faith decides the buying in six markets, and they've almost nothing else in common.
- One of those buyers is frightened of the post. Another is answering a calling. Same faith.
- "Christian life coach" tells a stranger your beliefs and nothing about what you fix.

## The six faith markets

**Christian and faith-based spirituality.** Under spirituality and meaning. The buyer is already inside a faith tradition and isn't looking to be convinced of anything. They want peace, grounding and a sense of purpose. Doctrine and community carry more weight here than any credential you hold.

**Faith-based debt freedom.** Under getting out of debt. The drive is fear, not ambition. They want out fast, without going deeper, and they want the discipline to stop it happening again. This market follows named teachers inside a church community. So belonging is the strongest lever in it.

**Faith-based adoption and orphan care.** Under adoption and foster care. The buyer is answering a calling and wants to do it well. The drive is protecting a child, with the approval of their community close behind.

**Faith and reconciling sexuality.** Under LGBTQ and coming out. The conflict here is reconciling a faith and a sexuality without losing God, the community, or themselves. Telling people is the easy half. That's a different job. And a coach who treats it as coming out will lose them.

**Religious and [scrupulosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrupulosity) OCD.** Under OCD and intrusive thoughts. They want relief from religious and moral obsessions without losing their faith in the process, which is the fear that keeps them out of ordinary therapy.

**Faith and recovery.** Under bipolar disorder. Faith is added to a clinical picture rather than replacing it. They want meaning and stability alongside managing the condition.

## Why the parent market counts for more than the faith

Because they share the faith and they don't share the problem.

Take two of them. There's a man who's frightened of the post, who hasn't opened the last three envelopes, and who wants out from under it without borrowing again. And there's a couple who've decided to adopt because they believe they're meant to, and who want to do it properly.

Both Christian. Both would read a page saying "Christian life coaching" and find nothing on it about them.

Write "faith-based coaching for Christians" on your homepage and you've said what you believe and nothing about what you fix. Same hole as "I help women over 40", with a different word sitting in it.

## What this means for your page

Name the market first and the faith second.

"Getting out of debt, for Christian families who want to do it without borrowing again." That tells the man with the unopened envelopes what you do and whose language you'll do it in. He knows from one line whether to carry on reading.

"Christian life coach" tells him half of it and leaves him guessing the rest. He won't guess. He'll go back to the search results.

One exception. If you work in spirituality and meaning itself, then the faith is the market, so naming it does say what the job is. That's one of the six, not all six. If that's you, [the spiritual coach website post](/blog/spiritual-coach-website) shows how to describe it in plain words.

## More Christian life coaching niches to consider

The six above are where faith decides the purchase. But there are plenty more places a Christian coach can work, where faith shapes how you coach and the market decides what people buy. Here are 20 ideas. Each one is a market first, with the faith layered on top.

**Relationships and family**

1. **Christian marriage coaching.** Couples who want to stop the same argument happening every week.
2. **Premarital coaching.** Engaged couples who want to start well, often through their church.
3. **Christian parenting coaching.** Parents of teenagers who've stopped talking to them.
4. **Faith-based divorce recovery.** People rebuilding after a divorce they never thought would happen to them.
5. **Christian singles and dating.** Singles who want a partner who shares their faith.
6. **Empty nest coaching.** Mums and dads working out who they are once the house goes quiet.

**Work, calling and money**

7. **Career and calling coaching.** People who feel stuck in a job and want work that fits their faith.
8. **Faith-based business coaching.** Christian business owners who want to grow without dropping their values.
9. **Financial stewardship.** Families who want a budget they can stick to and room to give.
10. **Second-half and retirement calling.** People in their fifties and sixties asking what's next.

**Church and ministry**

11. **Pastor coaching.** Pastors who are worn out and can't say so to their congregation.
12. **Ministry leader coaching.** Leaders of teams, volunteers and church plants.
13. **Pastors' spouses.** The person beside the pastor, carrying a load nobody sees.
14. **Missionary transition.** People heading out, or coming home and struggling to settle.

**Life stages and hard times**

15. **Grief with a faith lens.** People who've lost someone and are wrestling with God about it.
16. **Caregivers.** People looking after a parent or partner who need strength to keep going.
17. **Young adults.** Students and graduates trying to work out their direction.
18. **Christian women's identity and purpose.** Women in mid-life rethinking where they're putting their time.
19. **Christian men's coaching.** Men who want to lead well at home and at work.
20. **Faith-based health coaching.** People who want to look after their body as part of their faith.

**And for creative and public work**

21. **Christian authors and speakers.** People with a message who want to get it written or heard.

None of these are proven markets in our data the way the six above are. They're ideas, and every one of them works the same way: name the problem first and the faith second.

## How do you choose a Christian coaching niche?

1. **Start with who you've already helped.** The people who come to you at church or in your community already tell you what you're good at.
2. **Pick the problem, then the faith.** "Marriage coaching for Christian couples" beats "Christian coaching" every time.
3. **Check people will pay.** Look for books, courses and groups on that problem. If people already spend money on it, there's a market.

[How to choose a coaching niche](/blog/how-to-choose-a-coaching-niche) goes into this in more detail.

## FAQ

### Is Christian life coaching a niche?

Not on its own. It's a frame that sits on top of a market, and the market is what decides what the buyer wants. We found faith as the deciding factor in six different markets.

### What is the biggest Christian coaching niche?

Of the six, faith-based spirituality comes under the largest parent market, with 207 books behind spirituality and meaning. That also makes it the one with the most existing voices in it.

### Should I say I'm a Christian coach on my homepage?

Say what you help with first. The faith is how you'll do the work and whose language it will be in, and it belongs on the page. It just doesn't work as the only thing on it.

### How much does a Christian life coach make?

It depends far more on the niche, the price and the number of clients than on the faith. A Christian coach selling marriage coaching packages earns the same way any coach does. [How much life coaches make](/blog/how-much-do-life-coaches-make) goes through the numbers.

### What do you need to become a Christian life coach?

Coaching isn't a licensed job, so there's no legal requirement. Many Christian coaches do faith-based coach training, and some churches ask for it. What clients look for most is that you've helped people with their exact problem.

### Do these buyers want a Christian coach or a coach who is Christian?

It depends which of the six. In faith-based spirituality and in scrupulosity OCD, the faith is inside the problem itself. In debt freedom and adoption, the community matters more than the coach's own faith does.
