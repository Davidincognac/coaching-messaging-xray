Christian coaching is not a niche. It is a frame that changes what the buyer wants, and it appears in markets that otherwise have nothing to do with each other.

We mapped [117 coaching niches and 918 subniches](/blog/2026-09-29-coaching-niches) from what buyers in each already pay for.

Faith came back as the deciding factor in six of them. Six different parent markets.

That is the useful part. Not "Christian coaching is a niche", which tells you nothing.

But that a Christian buyer in one of these markets wants something a Christian buyer in another does not.

## The six faith markets

**Christian and faith-based spirituality.** Under spirituality and meaning. The buyer is already inside a faith tradition and is not looking to be convinced of anything. They want peace, grounding and a sense of purpose. Doctrine and community carry more weight here than any credential you hold.

**Faith-based debt freedom.** Under getting out of debt. The drive is fear, not ambition. They want out fast, without going deeper, and they want the discipline to stop it happening again. This market follows named teachers inside a church community. So belonging is the strongest lever in it.

**Faith-based adoption and orphan care.** Under adoption and foster care. The buyer is answering a calling and wants to do it well. The drive is protecting a child, with the approval of their community close behind.

**Faith and reconciling sexuality.** Under LGBTQ and coming out. The conflict is not disclosure. It is reconciling a faith and a sexuality without losing God, the community, or themselves. That is a different job. And a coach who treats it as coming out will lose them.

**Religious and scrupulosity OCD.** Under OCD and intrusive thoughts. They want relief from religious and moral obsessions without losing their faith in the process, which is the fear that keeps them out of ordinary therapy.

**Faith and recovery.** Under bipolar disorder. Faith is added to a clinical picture rather than replacing it. They want meaning and stability alongside managing the condition.

## Why the parent market matters more than the faith

Because the faith is shared and the job is not.

Take two of them. The debt-freedom buyer is trying to stop being frightened of the post. The adoption buyer is trying to do a good job of something they chose.

Both are Christian. And both would read a page about "Christian life coaching" and find nothing in it about them.

Write "faith-based coaching for Christians" on your homepage and you have named the frame and left the job blank.

Same mistake as "I help women over 40". Different word doing the hiding.

## What this means for your page

Name the market first and the faith second.

"Getting out of debt, for Christian families who want to do it without going deeper" says both. It tells the reader what the work is, and whose language it will be in.

"Christian life coach" says one. And leaves the reader guessing at the other.

I want to be fair about the exception. If you are working in spirituality and meaning itself, the faith is the market, and naming it is naming the job. That is one of the six, not all of them.

## Where the evidence stops

Six is what the reading supported, not a complete map of faith in coaching.

Our evidence is what buyers already spend money on in book form. And faith-based publishing leans towards a handful of traditions, so what came back leans the same way.

Everything here is about Christian buyers specifically, because that is what the evidence covered. I would rather say that than imply it generalises to faiths we did not read.

## FAQ

### Is Christian life coaching a niche?

Not on its own. It is a frame that sits on top of a market, and the market is what decides what the buyer wants. We found faith as the deciding factor in six different markets.

### What is the biggest Christian coaching niche?

Of the six, faith-based spirituality sits under the largest parent market, with 207 books behind spirituality and meaning. That also makes it the one with the most existing voices in it.

### Should I say I am a Christian coach on my homepage?

Say what you help with first. The faith is how you will do the work and whose language it will be in, and it belongs on the page. It just does not work as the only thing on it.

### Do these buyers want a Christian coach or a coach who is Christian?

It depends which of the six. In faith-based spirituality and in scrupulosity OCD, the faith is inside the problem itself. In debt freedom and adoption, the community matters more than the coach's own faith does.
