There isn't a life coach salary as such, because life coaches don't usually have an employer paying them a wage, they charge clients. The best public figures come from the International Coaching Federation's survey, which put average yearly income from coaching at $67,800 in North America, $52,400 in Western Europe and $52,800 worldwide. Those are averages before costs, from coaches who chose to answer, and plenty earn far less.

So that's the headline. The more useful answer to "how much do life coaches make" is a sum you can do yourself on the back of an envelope, and I'll show you it with a made-up coach further down.

This isn't financial advice, by the way. I'm a marketing bloke, not an accountant.

## Things to know

- There's no salary as such, because life coaches usually charge clients rather than draw a wage.
- The ICF's 2023 study (2022 figures) puts average income from coaching at $52,800 a year globally and $67,800 in North America.
- Those are averages from a survey of coaches, before tax and costs, and a few high earners pull an average up.
- Your income comes down to hours sold multiplied by price, minus costs, and getting clients is the hard part of that sum.

## How much do life coaches make, according to the ICF?

The best source I could find is the ICF Global Coaching Study. The ICF is an international body for coaches, and every couple of years they survey coaches around the world with PwC. The [2023 executive summary](https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2023/04/2023ICFGlobalCoachingStudy_ExecutiveSummary.pdf) covers 2022, and it's built on 14,591 responses from 157 countries. Here's what it says coaches earned from coaching, on average, in US dollars.

| Region | Average yearly income from coaching | Average fee for a one-hour session |
|---|---|---|
| North America | $67,800 | $272 |
| Oceania | $58,800 | $259 |
| Western Europe | $52,400 | $277 |
| Asia | $33,900 | $226 |
| Middle East and Africa | $29,600 | $165 |
| Latin America and the Caribbean | $22,900 | $114 |
| Eastern Europe | $18,700 | $138 |
| Global | $52,800 | $244 |

There's a newer one. The [2025 study](https://coachingfederation.org/research/global-coaching-study) says there are now 122,974 coach practitioners worldwide and that coaching brought in an estimated $5.34 billion over the year. If you divide one by the other you get roughly $43,000 a coach. That's my sum, not theirs, and the full 2025 report costs money, so I haven't seen how they break it down by region.

It's also been around this level for years. Back in 2017 the US Bureau of Labor Statistics wrote that North American coaches were reporting [about $61,900 a year from coaching](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2017/youre-a-what/life-coach.htm), and that's from an older ICF study too.

## Why the life coach salary figures look higher than they feel

I don't want you reading $67,800 and thinking you're failing if you're nowhere near it. There are good reasons those numbers sit higher than the life you see around you.

First, it's a survey. The coaches who fill in a long ICF questionnaire are more likely to be established, trained and busy. The person who did a weekend course, coached three friends and went back to their day job probably didn't answer.

Second, it's an average. If one coach earns $400,000 from corporate work and nine earn $15,000 each, the average is $53,500 and not one of those ten people earns anything like it.

Third, it's money in, not money kept. Income from coaching here means before tax, before your website, your training, your insurance and your software.

Fourth, the ICF figures cover more than life coaches. They count executive coaches, leadership coaches and business coaches too, and a lot of that work is paid for by companies. I've no experience of company-paid coaching myself, so I won't pretend to know what it pays, but I'd be surprised if it didn't pull the average up.

And fifth, the BLS piece makes a point I think gets missed. It says few coaches work only as coaches, and that many work part-time or have another job. So for plenty of coaches, "income from coaching" may well be a second income rather than a living.

The BLS also says it doesn't collect figures on life coaches specifically. So when you see a website quoting a precise life coach salary for your state or city, ask where it came from, because the government doesn't count it. In my opinion nobody tracks it properly.

## What actually decides how much a life coach makes?

A sum. It goes like this.

**Income = number of paid hours × price per hour, minus costs**

It looks obvious, but it's worth sitting with, because every bit of advice you'll ever get about coaching income is really about one of those three things. Raise your price. Sell more hours. Cut your costs. Notice what the sum doesn't contain, though. It says nothing about how good a coach you are.

The ICF figures show you one more thing. Alongside the $244 average session fee, the 2023 summary also gives an "average hourly revenue recovered" of $85 globally. I'm not completely sure how they worked it out, but I read it as the money spread over all the hours coaches put in, not just the paid ones. If I'm right, that's the difference between what you charge and what you actually take home for your time, and it's big.

## A worked example: meet Dan (he's made up)

Dan is a life coach in the US who helps men in their forties after a divorce. He's made up, and so are his numbers.

**Year one.** Dan charges $120 a session. He's working full time in his old job and coaching in the evenings. He averages 4 paid sessions a week over 46 weeks, because people go on holiday and so does he.

4 × $120 × 46 = $22,080 coming in.

His costs are a website, a scheduling tool, insurance, a bit of training and some ads he tried. Call it $4,000. So he's left with about $18,000 before tax. Not bad on the side, nowhere near a living.

**Year three.** Dan stops selling single sessions and sells a 12-week package for $1,800 instead. He's got some testimonials now, and he's put the price on his site. He sells 2 packages a month.

2 × 12 × $1,800 = $43,200 coming in.

That's nearly double, and look at what changed. His coaching is the same. What's different is that people can see what they're buying and what it costs, and he's found a steady way to get in front of men going through a divorce. He's doing more sessions, about 288 a year against 184, but each one now earns him $150 instead of $120.

**What Dan would need for $67,800.** About 38 packages a year, which is roughly 3 new clients every month, every month. Can it be done? Yes. But you can see the real job in that sentence, and the coaching is the easy part of it. The hard part is finding 3 new clients a month who'll pay $1,800.

So when people ask me how much life coaches make, my honest answer is that it depends on how many people know who you are and what you do, far more than on your training.

## FAQ

### What is the average life coach salary?

There isn't a salary, because life coaches usually charge clients rather than draw a wage. The ICF's 2023 study found average yearly income from coaching of $67,800 in North America, $52,400 in Western Europe and $52,800 worldwide, based on 2022 figures. That's before tax and costs.

### How much do life coaches make per hour?

The ICF's 2023 study put the average fee for a one-hour session at $244 globally and $272 in North America. That's what coaches charge, not what they earn for every hour they work, because marketing, admin and unpaid time all come out of it.

### Can you make a living as a life coach?

Yes, but plenty of people don't. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics says few coaches work only as coaches and many work part-time or have another job. Whether you make a living comes down to how many paying clients you can find at your price.

### Do life coaches need a qualification to earn money?

No. The BLS says there are no formal requirements for becoming a life coach. Training can help you coach better and help some clients trust you, but it doesn't bring clients to you on its own.
