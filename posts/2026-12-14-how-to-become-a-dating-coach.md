Let me start with a made-up person, because she'll explain this better than I can.

Her name's Sam. She's 38, she's been single for four years, and it's a Sunday night. She's just deleted the dating apps for the third time this year. She's not heartbroken exactly. She's just tired. She types "dating coach" into Google, feeling a bit daft for doing it, and opens three websites.

The first one has a bloke in a sharp suit promising to teach her "the psychology of attraction" so she can "become irresistible". She closes it in about four seconds. The second one says "Find the love you deserve" over a stock photo of a couple on a beach, and then there's a page of the coach's qualifications. She scrolls, can't work out what the coach actually does, and closes that too.

The third one says, at the top: "Fed up with the apps and starting to wonder if it's you? It isn't." Underneath there's a woman about her age saying she met her partner six months after starting coaching, without going back on the apps. Sam reads the whole page.

That's the whole job of a dating coach's marketing, really. If you want to become a dating coach, you need to be the third website. Here's how.

## Who actually hires a dating coach?

People who are worn out, not people who are desperate. That's an important difference, and a lot of dating coaches get it wrong.

When we looked at the books people buy about dating and what they write about it, the strongest feeling wasn't panic. It was exhaustion. People wrote "I'm really tired of constantly trying to find love" and "why is finding love so hard?" They wrote about cycling through the apps and feeling empty, or giving up on dating altogether. And a lot of them were embarrassed even to be asking. One person wrote "I feel so pathetic for even asking this question but it's eating me up".

So the person landing on your website is tired, a bit ashamed and has probably been let down by every app and piece of advice they've tried. That tells you exactly how to talk to them. Gently, without any shame, and without promising them the moon.

There's also a group right next door that's worth knowing about. Being single and happy about it had 40 books in our research, slightly more than dating and finding love, which had 37. These are people who are sick of hearing "what's wrong with you?" and want to feel fine on their own, whether or not they meet someone. Some dating coaches work with both, and it's worth deciding early whether you will.

## What's wrong with how dating coaching is usually sold?

Two things, in my opinion, and they're at opposite ends.

At one end there's the "high value" crowd. The pick-up lines, the "psychology of attraction", the promise that you can make anyone want you if you just learn the tricks. I can see why it sells to some people, but for the tired person typing "dating coach" on a Sunday night it's the opposite of what they want. They don't want tricks. They want to stop feeling like there's something wrong with them.

At the other end there's the soft-focus stuff. "Find the love you deserve." "Attract your soulmate." It's kinder, but it's so vague that nobody can tell what you'd actually do together. Is it a pep talk? Help with your profile? Someone to go through your dates with you afterwards?

The space in the middle is where the good dating coaches are. Honest, warm, specific about what you do, and with real stories from people who found someone or found peace with being single.

## Who are you going to help?

Pick one type of person and talk to them first. "People who want to find love" is everyone, so it's nobody. Some examples of groups that want different things:

- **Women in their late 30s and 40s who are done with the apps** and want a different way to meet someone.
- **People dating again after a divorce**, who haven't been on a first date in twenty years and don't know the rules any more.
- **Men who are nervous on dates** and keep getting "you're lovely, but".
- **People who keep picking the same wrong person** and want to break the pattern.

Each one of those needs a different headline, different testimonials and probably a different programme. If you've lived one of them yourself, that's usually the one to start with. And if you're drawn to working with people stuck in the same pattern, be aware it can overlap with attachment and relationship patterns, which is a busy area with plenty of quizzes and labels already out there. People in it want help changing the pattern, not another explanation of it.

## What will make people believe you?

Success stories. More than anything else.

When we mapped what drives buying decisions across 918 different types of coaching buyer, proof from other people helped the decision in 92.9% of them. For dating that's even more obvious. The tired person reading your page has one question: has this actually worked for someone like me? Show them it has.

The trouble is that two thirds of the coaching websites we looked at showed no proof at all. We looked at 10,954 live coaching websites and checked each homepage for 10 things a stranger needs before they'll get in touch, and proof was one of the things missing the most.

For a dating coach, good proof sounds like this:

- "I'd been on the apps for five years. Six months after starting with Jo I met my partner at a friend's party. We've just moved in together." *Rachel, 41*
- "I didn't meet anyone, and that's fine. I stopped feeling like a failure for being single, and I'm happier now than I've been in years." *Mark, 36*
- "I used to freeze on first dates. Now I actually enjoy them." *Tom, 29*

Those are made up to show you the shape. Notice the second one isn't even about finding a partner, and it's still strong proof, because it's honest. Use first names and ages if people don't want their full name out there, and always get permission. [The testimonials post](/blog/coaching-testimonials) goes into how to collect them.

## Do you need a qualification to become a dating coach?

No. Coaching isn't regulated in the UK or the US, so you can call yourself a dating coach without a licence. Training can help, especially with the coaching skills themselves and knowing when someone needs a therapist rather than a coach. But in dating, people care far more about whether you get it and whether it's worked for people like them.

When we looked at what people buying coaching respond to, authority came up in 87.9% of the 918 types of buyer. That's the sense that you've seen their problem before. A certificate is the weakest version of it. Having been back on the dating scene at 45 after a divorce, and come out the other side, is a much stronger one.

## What should a dating coach's website say?

Start with the tired person's own words. We found only 14.3% of coaching websites could be understood by a stranger within five seconds, so you've got a real chance to stand out just by being clear.

Here's the top of a homepage for a made-up dating coach who works with women done with the apps. It's an example to show you the shape, so write yours in your own clients' words.

> **Fed up with the apps and starting to wonder if it's you? It isn't.**
>
> I help women in their late 30s and 40s meet someone without spending every evening swiping. We work out what you actually want, where people like that are, and how to be yourself on a date instead of performing.
>
> *"Six months after starting with Jo I met my partner at a friend's party."* Rachel, 41
>
> Twelve weeks, six one-to-one sessions and a debrief after every date. From £X.
>
> Book a free 20-minute chat, or get my free guide: *"Five places to meet someone that aren't an app."*

Run through that as Sam. Is it for me? Yes, straight away. Has it worked for someone like me? Yes. What would we actually do? It says. What does it cost? Roughly. What do I do if I'm not ready to talk to anyone? There's a free guide. That's everything she needs to stay on the page.

Two things to notice. It doesn't promise she'll meet someone, because nobody honest can. And it talks about not using the apps, because the people in our research kept saying they wanted to "meet a significant other without going on dating sites". If that's what they want, say you can help with it.

## How should a dating coach price things?

As a package with a clear end point, not single sessions. Somebody who's tired of dating doesn't want to pay for an hour of chat and then decide whether to book another. They want to know what happens over the next few months. [The coaching offer post](/blog/coaching-offer) shows you how to lay that out, and [the coaching pricing post](/blog/coaching-pricing) covers whether to show the price on your website.

And please don't use countdown timers and "only two spots left". In our research, urgency helped the buying decision in just 4.4% of the types of buyer we mapped. For someone who already feels a bit embarrassed about hiring a dating coach, pressure is the fastest way to lose them.

## FAQ

### Do I need a qualification to be a dating coach?

Not by law, because coaching isn't regulated in the UK or the US. Training will help with your coaching skills and with knowing when to refer someone to a therapist. But clients will care far more about your own experience and the stories of people you've helped.

### Can a dating coach promise clients will find a partner?

No, and the honest ones don't. You can promise what you'll do together and what tends to change. Some of your best testimonials might come from people who didn't meet anyone but stopped feeling bad about being single.

### What's the difference between a dating coach and a matchmaker?

A matchmaker introduces you to people. A dating coach helps you work out what you want, where to find it and how to be yourself when you do. Some people want one, some want the other, so say clearly on your website which one you are.
