To start a financial coaching business, pick one money problem for one group of people, stay clearly on the coaching side of the advice line, build one package with a price, do the client sum, and go where your group already talks about money. The line between coaching and regulated financial advice is the thing that sets this niche apart, so get that clear before anything else.

![A couple at their kitchen table going through a paper budget and a calculator with a coach sitting opposite them](how-to-start-a-financial-coaching-business.jpg)

If you're still deciding whether to train, [how to become a financial coach](/blog/how-to-become-a-financial-coach) covers that side.

## Things to know

- Financial coaching is about behaviour: budgets, spending habits, debt plans, talking about money.
- Recommending specific investments, pensions or financial products is regulated advice in the US and UK.
- Pick one money problem. "Help with money" is too vague to sell.
- Buyers in 92.9% of the coaching markets we mapped decide on what other people say. Proof sells this niche.
- This is general information, not legal or financial advice.

## Step 1: know the advice line

This comes first because it decides what you can sell.

Financial coaches help people change what they do with money. Budgeting, getting out of debt, building savings habits, sorting money arguments with a partner. That's coaching.

Telling someone which fund to buy, which pension to move into or which insurance product to take out is a different job. In the US, giving investment advice for pay can require registration as an investment adviser. In the UK, financial advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Coaches who stray into it can get into real trouble.

A simple test: if you're naming a product or telling someone what to invest in, stop. Refer them to a regulated adviser. Put a line in your contract that coaching isn't financial advice. There's a [coaching contract template](/blog/coaching-contract-template) you can start from.

## Step 2: pick one money problem for one group

"I help people with their finances" is too wide. Pick something like:

- couples who argue about money and want one plan
- freelancers whose income goes up and down every month
- people paying off credit card debt for the first time

Those are people you can find, with problems they describe in their own words. "I never know if I can afford to pay myself this month" is a freelancer talking. Write your page in those words. [How to choose a coaching niche](/blog/how-to-choose-a-coaching-niche) helps if you're stuck between a few.

## Step 3: build one package and price it

One offer with a start and an end. A made-up example: "Debt-free plan in 8 weeks", with four sessions, a budget you build together and a check-in at the end.

Pricing is awkward in this niche, because your buyers are worried about money. A payment plan helps a lot. So does being clear about what they get. Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, 82.3% show no price at all, and for someone already anxious about money, a hidden price is a reason to leave. [Coaching pricing](/blog/coaching-pricing) covers how to show it.

## Step 4: do the sum

Income you want, plus costs, divided by price. If your package is £500 and you want £25,000 a year with £2,000 in costs, you need 54 clients a year, about 4 or 5 a month. If that feels like too many, raise the price, make the offer bigger, or treat year one as part time. The [coaching business plan](/blog/coaching-business-plan) has a full example.

## Step 5: set up the basics

- Register the business where you live.
- Get professional liability insurance and tell the insurer exactly what you do.
- A way to book a call and take payment.
- A page that says who you help, what changes for them and what to do next.

## Step 6: find clients

Go where your group talks about money. Freelancer communities, parenting groups, local talks, workplace wellbeing schemes, and Facebook groups where people ask money questions. Answer questions properly before you sell anything.

Proof does a lot of the selling in this niche. People need to believe you won't judge them, and that someone like them got somewhere. A couple of honest testimonials, even from people you helped for free, will do more than a clever headline.

## How does this fit with starting any coaching business?

Financial coaching follows the same steps as any coaching business, with the advice line added. ## FAQ

### How do I start a financial coaching business?

Get clear on the line between coaching and regulated advice first. Then pick one money problem for one group, build one package with a price, do the client sum, set up registration, insurance and booking, and go where your group talks about money.

### Do I need a license to be a financial coach?

Financial coaching itself isn't a licensed job. But recommending specific investments or financial products can be regulated advice, which does need registration. Stay on the behaviour side and refer product questions to a regulated adviser.

### What's the difference between a financial coach and a financial adviser?

A financial coach helps you change money habits, like budgeting, paying off debt and saving. A financial adviser is regulated and can recommend specific investments, pensions and products.

### How much do financial coaches charge?

It varies a lot by niche and country. Many sell packages rather than single sessions. Work out your price from the client sum above rather than copying someone else's number.
