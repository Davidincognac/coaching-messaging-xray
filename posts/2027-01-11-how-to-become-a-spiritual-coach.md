Here's something I've noticed about people who want to become a spiritual coach. The ones who'd be good at it are often the ones who feel worst about it.

They've been through something. An awakening that turned their life upside down, a long dark patch, years of searching that finally led somewhere. Friends already come to them for advice. People say "you should do this for a living". And then they sit down to actually do it, and the doubts start. Is it wrong to charge for this? Who am I to guide anyone? Won't people think I'm a bit odd?

Meanwhile, the people with none of those doubts are out there charging thousands for "quantum soul activation". So the careful ones hold back, and the loud ones fill the space.

This post is for the careful ones. Below are six doubts I hear again and again from people thinking about becoming a spiritual coach, and a straight answer to each. If you can get past these, the rest is just learning how to run a small business.

## Doubt 1: "Is it wrong to charge for spiritual help?"

This is the big one, and it stops more people than all the others put together.

I get where it comes from. Plenty of spiritual traditions teach that wisdom should be given freely. And you've probably seen people in the spiritual world charging silly money for things that look a lot like a pep talk with candles. You don't want to be that.

But think about it this way. A yoga teacher charges for classes. A vicar gets paid. A counsellor charges by the hour. Nobody thinks they're cashing in on people's souls. They're being paid for their time, their training and the years it took them to get good at it. You're no different.

And here's the part people forget. If you don't charge, you can't do it properly. You'll fit it around a full-time job, give it whatever energy you've got left on a Thursday night, and help a handful of people a year. Charging is what lets you give people your full attention.

What you can do, if it sits badly with you, is give plenty away too. Free writing, a free monthly group, a sliding scale for people who can't afford your full price. That way nobody's locked out, and you can still pay your rent.

## Doubt 2: "Who am I to guide anyone?"

Coaching isn't regulated in the UK or the US, so you don't need a licence to call yourself a spiritual coach. There are courses, and some are worth doing, especially for the coaching skills themselves. But no certificate is going to make you feel qualified to help someone with the biggest questions in their life. That feeling comes from somewhere else.

It comes from having been where they are. Someone in the middle of a spiritual awakening that's left them feeling, in the words of one person in my research, "peaceful but also lonely, weird, and sad", doesn't want a guru who's floated above it all. They want someone who's been lonely, weird and sad too, and found their way through.

So your own story is your qualification, as long as you're honest about it. You don't have to have all the answers. You have to be a few steps further down the same road, and be willing to walk some of it with them.

## Doubt 3: "Spiritual is so broad. Who would I even help?"

This is where my research can actually help you. When we looked at the books people buy about spirituality and what they write about it, it was one of the biggest subjects in the whole study, with 207 books. But underneath that, it splits into some very different groups:

- **Finding meaning and purpose**, 70 books. People who've got a decent life and still feel something's missing.
- **Spiritual self-care and inner peace**, 45 books. People who feel the world is "chaotic and overwhelming" and want to feel grounded.
- **Spiritual awakening**, 19 books. People something has happened to, and who don't know what to make of it.
- **Christian and faith-based spirituality**, 18 books. People who want their faith at the centre of the help they get.
- **Meditation and mindfulness**, 14 books. People who've tried to meditate and keep failing. "I try and I can't focus."
- **Eastern philosophy and non-duality**, 13 books.
- **Energy healing**, 5 books, and **manifestation and the law of attraction**, 4 books. People asking "why isn't the law of attraction working for me?"

Each of those people would want something different from you. The person whose awakening has left them lonely doesn't need help manifesting. The person who can't focus during meditation doesn't need a talk about purpose.

So pick one group to start with, usually the one you came through yourself. You can help others later. But if you're Christian and want to coach people of faith, [the Christian life coaching niches post](/blog/christian-life-coaching-niches) goes into that in more detail.

## Doubt 4: "I hate the idea of selling"

Good. The people who'll hire you hate being sold to, so you're already on the same side.

I spent years marketing driving schools, and there's a lesson from that which fits here. Nobody ever bought driving lessons because of an advert about "freedom on the open road". They bought them because they were 17, they'd failed their test once, and they wanted someone patient who'd get them through it. The driving instructors who did well were the ones who described the problem exactly as the learner felt it.

It's the same for you. Selling, done properly, isn't persuading someone to buy something they don't want. It's describing their problem so clearly that they think "that's me", then telling them plainly what you'd do about it and what it costs. No countdown timers. No "only three spaces left". In our research, urgency helped the buying decision in just 4.4% of the types of buyer we mapped. For spiritual seekers, pressure is the quickest way to make them run.

## Doubt 5: "Won't people think I'm a bit woo?"

Some will. That's ok. You're not for everyone.

But a lot of the "woo" reaction comes from language, not from the work itself. When a website says "Raise your vibration and step into your soul's highest alignment", a stranger switches off, even a spiritual one. When it says "If your awakening has left you feeling out of step with everyone you love, I can help you make sense of it", that same stranger leans in.

The work can be as spiritual as you like. The words on the front of your website have to make sense to someone who's never been to a spiritual coach before. [The spiritual coach website post](/blog/spiritual-coach-website) shows you how to translate the usual phrases into words your clients actually use.

## Doubt 6: "What if someone needs more than I can give?"

They will, at some point. Some of the people who come to a spiritual coach are going through a [spiritual crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_crisis) that overlaps with serious mental health problems. Some are dealing with depression, trauma or grief that needs a therapist or a doctor, not a coach. The words people use in the spiritual world, like "the dark night of the soul" or "ego death", can sometimes describe something that needs proper medical help.

So decide where your line is before you take your first client. Have a couple of therapists you trust that you can point people to. And say on your website what you do and don't work with. It won't scare the right people off. It'll make them trust you more, because it shows you take what they're going through seriously.

## What a first offer could look like

Once you've got past the doubts, you need something to actually sell. Here's an example for a made-up spiritual coach who works with people whose awakening has left them feeling isolated. It's there to show you the shape, so build yours around your own people.

> **Making sense of it: six conversations over three months**
>
> Something's changed in you, and the people around you don't get it. Over six one-to-one conversations, every two weeks, we'll make sense of what's happened, find practices that actually help you feel grounded, and work out how to live this way without losing the people you love.
>
> You'll also get a short written summary after each conversation, and you can message me between sessions if something comes up.
>
> From £X. If that's out of reach right now, ask me about the sliding scale.

Notice what's in it. Who it's for, what happens, how often, what you get between sessions, roughly what it costs, and a way in for people who can't afford it. Notice what isn't. No promise of enlightenment, no "transformation", no "activation". [The coaching offer post](/blog/coaching-offer) shows you how to put one together, and [the coaching pricing post](/blog/coaching-pricing) covers whether to put the price on your website.

## Where your first clients will come from

From people who already trust you. The friends who already come to you for advice. The people from your meditation group, your church, your yoga class. People who've seen you go through it and come out the other side.

Tell them what you're doing, in one plain sentence. "I'm working with people whose awakening has left them feeling lonely, to help them make sense of it." Ask if they know anyone. Offer your first few people a lower price in return for honest feedback and a testimonial. [How to get your first coaching client](/blog/first-coaching-client) goes into this step by step.

## FAQ

### Do I need to be religious to become a spiritual coach?

No. Plenty of spiritual coaches don't follow any religion, and plenty do. What clients care about is whether you understand their experience and respect their beliefs. If you work within one faith, say so clearly, because people who share it will look for that.

### Can I become a spiritual coach alongside my day job?

Yes, and plenty of people start that way. It takes the pressure off while you find your first clients and work out what you're good at. Just be honest with yourself about how much time you've actually got, and don't take on more clients than you can give your full attention to.

### What's the difference between a spiritual coach and a spiritual director?

Spiritual direction usually comes from a religious tradition, often Christian, and focuses on someone's relationship with God. A spiritual coach can come from any tradition or none, and usually focuses on helping someone make changes in their life. Some people do both.
