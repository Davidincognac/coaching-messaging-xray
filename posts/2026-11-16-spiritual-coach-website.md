I want to start by saying I've got a lot of respect for spiritual coaches. You're working with the part of people's lives that's hardest to put into words, and plenty of your clients turn up not even sure what they're looking for. That's a hard job.

But that's exactly why so many spiritual coaching websites don't work. The thing you do is hard to describe, so the website reaches for the words the whole spiritual world uses. Awaken. Align. Your highest self. Soul purpose. Step into your power. And a stranger reads all that and still has no idea what happens if they book a session with you.

## Why is this a bigger problem for spiritual coaches than anyone else?

Because your clients often can't name what they need yet. A career coach's client knows they want a new job. A spiritual coach's client might just feel that something's off.

We looked at the books people buy and what they write about them across 117 different reasons people hire a coach. Spirituality and meaning was one of the biggest subjects, with 207 of the 2,004 books we looked at. And it stood out for one reason. Of all the people we looked at, these were among the least likely to know there's even a name for what they need. They don't search for "spiritual coach". They write things like "the world feels chaotic and overwhelming", or that their awakening has left them feeling "peaceful but also lonely, weird, and sad".

So if your website opens with "Awaken to your soul's purpose", you're using the words of someone who's already found the answer, to talk to someone who hasn't found the question yet. They don't recognise themselves in it, so they leave.

## What does the research say about coaching websites?

We looked at 10,954 live coaching websites and checked each homepage for 10 things a stranger needs before they'll get in touch. Things like whether you can tell who it's for, whether there's any proof, and whether there's a price. If you want the detail of how we checked, [it's here](/methodology).

Only 14.3% could be understood by a stranger within five seconds. And 49.2% weren't clear about who they help or what they help with. That's across all coaching. Spiritual coaching sites struggle with both even more, in my opinion, because the language makes it so easy to say a lot and mean very little.

## How do you translate spiritual language into plain words?

Take each phrase you use and ask what the person sitting opposite you would actually say. Not what you'd call it after years of training, what they'd say before they'd ever met you. Here's what that looks like for some of the phrases you'll see on almost every spiritual coaching site:

| What spiritual coaching websites say | What the client might actually say |
|---|---|
| Awaken to your true self | "I don't know who I am any more" |
| Align with your soul's purpose | "I've got a good life and I still feel empty" |
| Navigate your spiritual awakening | "Something shifted and now I feel lonely and a bit weird" |
| Raise your vibration | "I keep trying to manifest things and it isn't working" |
| Find inner peace through stillness | "I try to meditate but I can't stop my mind racing" |

The words on the right side are the ones that make someone stop scrolling, because they've thought that exact sentence. Some of them are close to things people actually wrote in my research. People using the law of attraction wrote about trying for months and it not working, and people learning to meditate wrote about trying and not being able to focus.

You don't have to drop your own language completely. It's how you think about the work, and some of your clients will love it. Just don't lead with it. Lead with their words, then bring yours in once they know the page is for them.

## What does that look like as a headline?

Here are three headlines for three different spiritual coaches, each written for one type of client. They're examples to show you the shape, so write yours in your own clients' words.

**For the person whose awakening has left them feeling alone:**
"Something's changed in you, and now you feel out of step with everyone around you. I help you make sense of it without losing the people you love."

**For the person who's tried manifesting and it isn't working:**
"You've done the journalling, the visualising and the affirmations for months, and nothing's shifted. Let's find out why."

**For the person who can't get meditation to work:**
"If you sit down to meditate and your mind just won't shut up, you're not doing it wrong. I'll show you what actually helps."

Every one of those says who it's for and what's wrong, in words the reader would use about themselves. None of them would put off a spiritual person, and all of them would make sense to someone who's never been to a spiritual coach in their life.

## What should come after the headline?

The same things any coaching website needs, with a slightly different weight on each.

**Your story does more work here than on any other kind of coaching site.** People hiring a spiritual coach are trusting you with something very personal, so they want to know who you are and why you do this. 90.7% of the coaching sites that did best had a story on the page, and a third of all coaching sites have nothing human on them at all. Don't be one of the third.

**Proof is harder, so make it specific.** Two thirds of coaching websites show no proof at all. Spiritual coaches often say their results can't be measured, and that's partly true. But a client saying "I stopped feeling like I was going mad" or "I can sit with my thoughts now without panicking" is proof a stranger understands. Ask your clients what changed, in their words, and use that.

**Say what a session actually is.** Spiritual coaching is a mystery to a lot of people. Is it talking? Meditation? Energy work? Something else? Tell them what happens in the room, or on the call, and what they'll walk away with. [The coaching offer post](/blog/coaching-offer) shows you how to set that out.

**Show a price, or explain what it depends on.** 82.3% of coaching websites don't show any price. [The coaching pricing post](/blog/coaching-pricing) goes through both options.

## Isn't plain language a bit unspiritual?

I'd say the opposite. Meeting someone where they are is about as spiritual as it gets. If the person who needs you can't understand your website, it makes no difference how beautiful the words are, because they'll never get as far as the first session.

## FAQ

### What should a spiritual coach put at the top of their website?

Who you help and what's going on for them, in the words they'd use themselves. "I don't know who I am any more" works better than "Awaken to your true self", because the person who needs you has thought the first one and has probably never said the second.

### Should a spiritual coach website use spiritual language at all?

Yes, but not first. Lead with your client's words so they know the page is for them, then bring in your own language once they're reading. Some clients will love it, but nobody should have to decode it to work out what you do.
