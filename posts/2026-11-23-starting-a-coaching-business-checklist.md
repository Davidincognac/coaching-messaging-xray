Starting a coaching business comes down to 15 jobs, and the order is what makes it work. Decide who you help and what you sell first. Then sort the legal and money basics. Then build the few things people see. Then go and talk to people. A lot of new coaches do it backwards and build the website first.

Below is the full list, with a line on why each one is there. If you want the longer walk-through of each stage, [how to start an online coaching business](/blog/how-to-start-an-online-coaching-business) goes through it step by step.

## Things to know

- Do the thinking steps (who, problem, offer, price) before you spend money on anything.
- The legal steps depend on where you live. This is a general list, not legal or tax advice.
- A website is step 10, not step 1. You can get your first clients without one.
- Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, 66.5% show no proof at all. Plan for proof from day one.
- The last step, checking your numbers every month, is the one that keeps the business alive.

## Stage 1: decide what you're selling

**1. Pick who you help.** One group of people you can actually find. "Women going through divorce in their 40s" is a group. "People who want more from life" isn't. [How to choose a coaching niche](/blog/how-to-choose-a-coaching-niche) helps if you're stuck.

**2. Write their problem in their words.** Not coaching words. If they say "I can't stop arguing with my ex about the kids", write that, not "co-parenting conflict".

**3. Make one offer.** One thing, with a start and a finish. Six sessions over ten weeks is something people can picture. "Ongoing support" isn't. [The coaching offer](/blog/coaching-offer) covers how to build it.

**4. Set a price.** Pick a number you can say out loud without flinching. [Coaching pricing](/blog/coaching-pricing) goes into how.

**5. Do the client sum.** The income you want, plus your costs, divided by your price. That's how many clients you need. If the answer scares you, you've found out cheaply. The [coaching business plan](/blog/coaching-business-plan) post shows the sum worked through.

## Stage 2: the legal and money basics

**6. Register the business.** In the UK that usually means registering as a sole trader for Self Assessment. In the US it depends on your state, and a lot of coaches start as a sole proprietor. [Does a life coach need a business license](/blog/does-a-life-coach-need-a-business-license) goes into the US side.

**7. Open a separate bank account.** Keeping coaching money apart from your own makes tax time far less painful.

**8. Get a simple contract.** Every client signs something that says what they get, what it costs and what happens if they cancel. There's a [coaching contract template](/blog/coaching-contract-template) on this blog.

**9. Sort insurance.** Professional liability (sometimes called professional indemnity) is the usual one for coaches. Ask a broker what fits where you are.

## Stage 3: the things people see

**10. One clear page.** A homepage or a single page that says who you help, what you fix and what to do next. That's enough to start. [What to put on a coaching homepage](/blog/what-to-put-on-a-coaching-homepage) gives the order.

**11. A way to book or pay.** A booking link and a way to take money. Don't make people email you to ask how.

**12. Proof, even a little.** A quote from someone you've helped, even for free. Out of 10,954 coaching websites we scored, 66.5% show no proof at all. Buyers in 92.9% of the coaching markets we mapped decide on what other people say. Get proof early.

## Stage 4: go and get clients

**13. Talk to 20 people.** Not a launch. Twenty real conversations with people who have the problem. You'll learn more from those than from any course. [How to get your first coaching client](/blog/first-coaching-client) covers where to start.

**14. Pick one place to find people, and stay there.** One. A Facebook group, local talks, LinkedIn, whatever fits your buyers. Spreading thin across five channels is how new coaches burn out.

## Stage 5: keep it going

**15. Check your numbers every month.** Conversations, calls booked, clients signed, money in, and where each client came from. Half an hour a month. This is how you find out what's working before you run out of money.

## What do people skip?

Steps 2, 5 and 15. Writing the problem in the buyer's words feels fiddly. The client sum feels scary. And checking numbers feels like admin. But those three are the ones that tell you if the business can work.

The one people do too early is step 10. I've worked with coaches since 2007, and the pattern hasn't changed. People spend months on a logo and a website before they've had one real conversation with a buyer. Then the website says the wrong thing, because they didn't know what their buyers say yet.

## Can I do this while working a job?

Yes, and plenty of coaches do. Stages 1 and 2 can be done in evenings. Stage 4 is where the time goes, because conversations take time. If you've only got a few hours a week, spend them talking to people, not polishing things.

## FAQ

### What do I need to start a coaching business?

Someone to help, a problem they'll pay to fix, one offer with a price, the legal basics where you live (registration, a contract, insurance), a way for people to book and pay, and a plan for where your first clients come from. A website helps but isn't the first thing.

### How long does it take to start a coaching business?

The setup can be done in a few weeks. Getting to steady clients takes longer, usually months, and depends on how many conversations you have. The checklist above gets you ready to start those conversations.

### Do I need a certification to start a coaching business?

Coaching isn't a licensed profession in the UK, and there's no state license for it in the US, so you don't legally need one to start. Some buyers like to see training, and some niches (health, money) have rules about what you can advise on. Check the rules for your niche.

### What's the first step in starting a coaching business?

Pick who you help. Everything else, the offer, the price, the words on your page and where you find clients, depends on that answer.
