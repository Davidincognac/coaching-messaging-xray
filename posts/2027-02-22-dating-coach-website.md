I could give you a list of rules for a dating coach website. Say who you help. Show proof. Put a price on it. You've probably read lists like that before, and they're right, but they're hard to picture.

So I'm going to do something different. I'm going to write the same homepage three times, for the same made-up dating coach. The first version is the kind you'll find all over the internet. The second is better. The third is the one I'd actually put live. After each one, I'll say what's wrong with it, or what's working, so you can see exactly what changed.

![A man in his 40s at a café table with a coffee, talking to someone across the table](dating-coach-cafe-man.jpg)

## Meet the coach

His name's Rob. He's 41, he was hopeless on dates in his twenties, and he worked out what he was doing wrong the slow way. Now he coaches men who get nervous on dates, who freeze up, talk too much or go blank, and who keep hearing some version of "you're lovely, but".

That's a real kind of person to help. When we looked at the books people buy about dating and what they write about it, the feeling that came through wasn't panic, it was being worn out. People wrote "I'm really tired of constantly trying to find love" and "why is finding love so hard?" And plenty of them were embarrassed even to be asking. One person wrote "I feel so pathetic for even asking this question but it's eating me up".

That embarrassment is the thing Rob's website has to get past. Let's see how each version does.

## Version 1: the one you see everywhere

> **UNLOCK YOUR DATING POTENTIAL**
>
> Hi, I'm Rob, a certified dating and attraction coach. I help men become the confident, high-value version of themselves and attract the partner they deserve.
>
> Using proven psychology and my signature ATTRACT Method™, I'll help you master the art of connection.
>
> **Services:** 1:1 Coaching | Profile Makeover | Date Debriefs | VIP Intensive
>
> **[Book a Discovery Call]**

### What's wrong with version 1

Pretty much everything, if I'm honest, but let's be specific.

**The headline says nothing.** "Unlock your dating potential" could be a dating app, a book or a hair product. A stranger would have no idea what Rob does within five seconds, or fifty.

**"High-value" puts off the very people Rob wants.** It's language from the pick-up world. A nervous man who's embarrassed to be looking at a dating coach reads "high-value" and thinks either "that's not me" or "I don't want to be that guy". Either way, he leaves.

**The method name means nothing to a stranger.** A trademarked acronym feels important to the coach. To the visitor, it's one more thing to work out.

**Four services and no explanation.** Which one is for him? What's a "VIP Intensive"? He's not going to click four links to find out.

**No proof, no price, and the only option is a call.** And "Book a Discovery Call" asks a nervous man to talk to a stranger about the thing that embarrasses him more than anything, with nothing in between.

## Version 2: better, but not there yet

> **Dating coaching for men who want to feel confident on dates**
>
> Do you get nervous on first dates? Struggle to know what to say? I help men build real confidence so they can be themselves and make a genuine connection.
>
> I'm Rob, and I've been coaching men for six years.
>
> *"Rob really helped me with my confidence. Highly recommend!" – J.*
>
> **[Book a free call]**

### What's better in version 2

Plenty. The headline says who it's for and roughly what it's about. "Do you get nervous on first dates?" is a question the right man would answer yes to. The method name and the four-box menu have gone. There's a testimonial. And the call is free.

### What's still wrong with version 2

**It's still a bit general.** "Feel confident on dates" is what every dating coach for men says. It doesn't describe anything the reader would recognise as his own experience.

**The testimonial is weak.** "Rob really helped me with my confidence" could be about anything. It doesn't say what changed.

**There's still no idea of what happens or what it costs.** He knows Rob helps with confidence. He has no idea what they'd actually do together, for how long, or for how much.

**Rob's own story is missing.** "I've been coaching men for six years" is a fact, not a reason to trust him. The thing that makes Rob believable is that he used to be exactly as bad on dates as the reader. Version 2 leaves that out.

## Version 3: the one I'd put live

> **Get to the end of a first date without your mind going blank.**
>
> If you get so nervous on dates that you either talk too much or freeze, and you keep hearing "you're lovely, but...", I can help. I was that bloke all through my twenties.
>
> **What we'll do**
>
> Eight weeks, six one-to-one video calls. We'll work out what actually happens to you on a date, practise the bits that go wrong, and I'll debrief every real date with you by message the same night.
>
> *"I used to dread the first ten minutes of a date. Last month I actually enjoyed one, and we're seeing each other again on Friday." – Dan, 34*
>
> *"I didn't realise how much I was interviewing them instead of talking. Fixing that changed everything." – Chris, 39*
>
> **£X for the eight weeks.** You can pay in two halves.
>
> **[Book a free 20-minute chat]** or, if you're not ready to talk to anyone yet, **[get my free guide: "The first ten minutes of a date"]**

### Why version 3 works

**The headline describes something he's felt.** "Your mind going blank" is a real moment, not a vague hope. A man who's sat across a table from someone with nothing in his head knows straight away this is for him.

**It says who it's for, in detail.** Talking too much, freezing, "you're lovely, but". If that's him, he's hooked. If it isn't, he leaves, and that's fine.

**Rob's story is in one short line.** "I was that bloke all through my twenties." No life story, just enough to say "I've been where you are".

**It says exactly what happens.** Eight weeks, six calls, practise, and a debrief after every real date. He can picture it.

**The proof names real moments.** "I used to dread the first ten minutes" and "I was interviewing them instead of talking" are specific and believable. Nobody writes that if it didn't happen. In our research, proof from other people helped the buying decision in 92.9% of the types of buyer we mapped. These two quotes are the kind that do it.

**There's a price.** He knows what he's getting into. No guessing, no "investment on request".

**There are two ways in.** The man who's ready can book a chat. The man who's embarrassed and not ready to talk can download a guide on his own, at midnight, without anyone knowing. That second button is often missing on dating coach websites, and it's the one embarrassed people need.

## The rest of the website

The homepage does the heavy lifting, but a dating coach website needs a few other pages too.

**About.** A longer version of your story, honestly told. What you were like, what you worked out, and why you help people now. Keep it about what you learned, not about your exes.

**Stories.** More testimonials, with first names and ages. Some about meeting someone, and some about feeling better on dates even before anyone special turned up. Always ask permission first, and check how each person wants to be named.

**How it works and what it costs.** The full details of your programme, what's included and how to pay.

**The free guide.** A simple page explaining what's in it, with a short form. Two boxes, first name and email, nothing more.

Notice there's no blog of 50 dating tips, no "media appearances" page and no shop. You can add those later. They won't get you your first clients.

## Which version is yours?

Read your own homepage out loud and be honest. If it sounds like version 1, start with the headline and the services menu. If it sounds like version 2, you're close. Make it more specific, add your own story in a line, and say what happens and what it costs.

## FAQ

### What should be on a dating coach's homepage?

A headline describing a moment your client recognises, a line on who you help, a short line about your own story, what you'll do together, two or three specific testimonials, a price, and two ways in: a free call and a free guide for people who aren't ready to talk.

### Should a dating coach use words like "high-value" or "attraction"?

Only if your clients use them. For nervous, worn-out people, words from the pick-up world often put them off. Use the words your clients use about themselves, like "nervous", "freeze" or "tired of the apps".

### Should a dating coach put their own photo on the website?

Yes. People are trusting you with something they find embarrassing, so they want to see who they'd be talking to. A friendly, normal photo of you works far better than a stock photo of a happy couple, which mostly reminds the reader of what they haven't got.

### Does a dating coach website need a free guide?

In my opinion, yes. Plenty of people looking at a dating coach are embarrassed and not ready to talk to anyone. A short free guide they can read on their own gives them a way in, and lets them get to know you first.
